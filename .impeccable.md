## Design Context

### Users
People who stutter, ages roughly 16–45, using the app privately — on the couch, before a job interview, during a quiet moment. The emotional register is high: these are people rebuilding their relationship with speech, which is personal and often loaded with anxiety. The app must feel like a trusted, calm companion — never clinical, never condescending.

### Brand Personality
**Calm, purposeful, earned confidence.** Three words: quiet, structured, courageous.

The app should feel like Linear's precision meets Headspace's warmth — every interaction signals "we thought about you." Nothing is decorative for its own sake. Every element earns its place.

Anti-references: generic health/wellness pastels, startup excitement, anything that feels patronizing or over-cheerful.

### Aesthetic Direction
**Light mode. Editorial minimalism with deliberate breathing room.**

- Background: warm off-white #FAF9F6 (not clinical white)
- Text primary: near-black #1A1A1A
- Primary accent: deep indigo #2D2A4A — dominant, confident, not aggressive
- Secondary accent: soft gold #C9A84C — used only as a whisper (liked state, single highlights)
- White surfaces (#FFFFFF / #FDFCFA) with soft, diffused shadows
- No coral, no orange, no terracotta — those colors were transitional

Typography: Epilogue (display/headers — confident grotesque, not a serif) + Figtree (body — warm, geometric, readable). Max 5 sizes. Generous line-height. Slight letter-spacing on headers.

Spacing: strict 8px grid, generous overall — premium apps breathe.

Motion: easeOutCubic or spring. Haptic feedback on key moments. Skeleton loading over spinners.

### Design Principles
1. **Earn every pixel.** If a decorative element doesn't serve the user's goal, remove it.
2. **Calm wins.** When in doubt, reduce intensity — less color, more space, quieter animation.
3. **One moment at a time.** Each screen has ONE primary action. Everything else supports it.
4. **Confidence through craft.** Polish is not decoration; it signals "we trust this."
5. **Gold is a reward.** The gold accent only appears when something good happens (a like, a completion) — it should feel like a small celebration.
